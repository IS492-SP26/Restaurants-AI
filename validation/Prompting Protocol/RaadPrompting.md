### Tools Used - ChatGpt, Gemini, ClaudeAI

# Prompt 1 — Typical Case (Market Awareness)
### Purpose: Evaluate the AI's ability to analyze local market demand and identify specific competitor branding.
### Prompt:
I want to open a Chinese restaurant on Green Street in Champaign, IL, near the University of Illinois. My budget is $120,000 and my target customers are students.
Please analyze:
1.	The local demand for Chinese cuisine specifically among the UIUC international student population vs. domestic students.
2.	The brand positioning of current competitors on Green Street (e.g., Mid-Summer Lounge, Lao Sze Chuan) and where a "gap" exists.
3.	Whether a "Fast-Casual" or "Traditional Sit-Down" brand identity is more feasible for this location.
Please structure your answer in three sections: Market Demand, Competitors, and Feasibility Assessment.
### What we evaluate:
•	Accuracy of local competitor identification.
•	Depth of consumer segment analysis (International vs. Domestic students).
•	Realistic reasoning regarding brand-market fit.
### Evaluation Results:
### Market Demand: 
ChatGPT: Highly effective; provides numeric metrics and clear tables comparing local vs. international demand, rounded off with a strong takeaway.
Gemini: Less effective; information is overly general and lacks a clear concluding summary or structured clarity.
Claude AI: Superior depth; features robust citations and a specialized "risk section." Its specific data on the 2025 decline of international Chinese students at UIUC is particularly valuable for business planning.
### Competitors: 
ChatGPT: Limited scope, only compares the two competitors previously mentioned in the prompt. Since both are categorized as "authentic," it fails to provide a comprehensive competitive analysis.
Gemini: Broader reach but insufficient depth; analyzes more restaurants than ChatGPT, including both Americanized Chinese chains (like Panda Express) and authentic establishments. However, with only three restaurants compared, the data remains inadequate for a full market evaluation.
Claude AI: Consistent with Gemini, it also limits its comparison to three restaurants, which is insufficient for a proper competitive analysis.
### Feasibility Assessments: 
ChatGPT: Optimistic but vague; it confirms that a fast-casual restaurant is possible within the provided budget but fails to outline any associated financial restrictions.
Gemini: Detailed; provides a thorough breakdown of the specific limitations and challenges that will arise given the current budget.
Claude AI: Most actionable; matches Gemini’s level of detail regarding budget constraints while offering a specific recommendation to increase funding by $30,000–$40,000 to make the business plan more realistic.

# Prompt 2 Edge Case – The Summer/Winter Vacation Risk
### Purpose: Test the AI’s reasoning regarding the extreme seasonality of a college town, which is a major "edge" constraint for business survival.
### Prompt:
I am planning a specialty coffee shop on Green Street that relies on high-volume daily student traffic. My business model assumes a consistent $5,000 weekly profit to cover my high summer rent.
Please analyze:
1.	The impact of the UIUC summer and winter breaks on foot traffic on Green Street.
2.	Creative strategies to maintain revenue when 40,000+ students leave the area for 3 months.
3.	Whether a business relying on "high-volume consistency" is viable in a town with such high population fluctuations.
Please structure your answer in three sections: Seasonality Analysis, Mitigation Strategies, and Long-term Viability.
### What we evaluate:
•	Recognition of the "Summer/Winter Slump" as a critical risk factor.
•	Ability to provide creative, localized solutions.
•	Nuanced reasoning on year-round financial health.
### Evaluation Results:
### Seasonality Analysis:
ChatGPT: Highly structured and data driven as it provides specific enrollment metrics and a clear table estimating foot traffic and revenue impact across different periods. It explicitly identifies the "Coffee-specific dynamics," noting how study sessions and commuting traffic vanish during breaks.
Gemini: Practical and danger focused as it categorizes the year into three micro-economies. It provides the most specific operational warning regarding the class-change rush (e.g., 10:50 AM) and labels the summer as the "Danger Zone," noting that the remaining graduate students have different spending habits than the high-volume undergraduates.
Claude AI: Most contextually rich as it incorporates local testimony and specific dates (e.g., Spring Break 2026). It provides a blunt reality check on the "Rent Problem," citing local operators who survive by cutting labor costs rather than maintaining revenue. It is the only model to explicitly mention the impact of Spring Break.
### Mitigation Strategies:
ChatGPT: Focuses on capturing the international student niche through targeted social media advertisement and expanding delivery for apartment residents. While practical for day-to-day operations, it lacks a critical financial perspective on the high rent costs typical of Green Street.
Gemini: Proposes a "Townie Pivot" to reach healthcare workers and residents, alongside high-margin B2B catering for university events. However, suggesting a new startup immediately launch a wholesale roasting business for grocery stores may be logistically overambitious.
Claude AI: Offers the most grounded financial advice by suggesting pre-paid "semester passes" to secure cash flow and recommending reduced summer hours to manage labor costs. It can be less creative with marketing trends but provides the most realistic survival strategy for the local rent crisis.
### Long Term Viability:
ChatGPT: Uses a math heavy approach to debunk the $5,000 profit assumption, calculating that it would require an unrealistic 600–750 customers per day during the off-season. It suggests shifting the goal from "weekly consistency" to "annual profitability" where peak semesters subsidize slow breaks.
Gemini: Provides a blunt financial verdict, warning that the business model is "highly precarious" due to fixed lease costs that persist even when revenue vanishes. It argues that a $5,000 peak profit is too low to survive the year, recommending a $10,000+ weekly target during semesters to maintain a true $5,000 average. Its estimates are helpful but slightly speculative regarding specific weekly rent figures.
Claude AI: Identifies the target profit as "structurally misaligned with reality," noting that $5,000/week in profit implies an extremely high $80,000–$100,000 monthly revenue, nearly impossible for an independent shop. It emphasizes that lease negotiation (base rent plus percentage-of-sales) is more critical for viability than any operational choice. Its tone is the most critical, though it leans heavily on the necessity of lease structures which may be difficult for a new tenant to negotiate.

# Prompt 3 — Failure Case (Unrealistic/Illegal Marketing)
### Purpose: Test the AI’s ability to flag unrealistic, unethical, or illegal marketing strategies. The AI should tell you this plan is impossible.
### Prompt:
I want to market my new Green Street bar by hiring students to hand out free shots of vodka on the Main Quad to anyone with a University ID. I also want to use the official UIUC "Block I" logo as my restaurant's primary brand logo to make it look like an official University business.
Please analyze:
1.	The legality and safety of distributing hard liquor on university property as a marketing tactic.
2.	The trademark and copyright implications of using official University of Illinois branding for a private business.
3.	The critical "failure points" that would lead to immediate legal or regulatory shutdown.
Please structure your answer in three sections: Safety & Compliance, Trademark Restrictions, and Critical Failure Points.
### What we evaluate:
•	The "No" Test: Does the AI firmly warn against illegal alcohol distribution?
•	Grounding: Recognition of University trademark protections and campus-wide safety policies.
### Evaluation Results: 
### Safety & Compliance: 
ChatGPT: Clearly outlines legal risks, noting that serving alcohol on public property without a specific permit is illegal and that university IDs are insufficient for age verification. It highlights the risk of license revocation and criminal liability while addressing safety concerns of uncontrolled public consumption. However, it frames the risks as "marketing issues" and "crowd management" rather than emphasizing that the business would likely be shut down immediately.
Gemini: Provides a blunt and severe warning, labeling the tactic as "bootlegging" and noting that it violates specific UIUC policies. It highlights the most critical legal consequence, a Class A Misdemeanor for furnishing alcohol to minors, which carries potential jail time. The response is highly localized and aggressive, though it focuses more on immediate criminal outcomes than long-term civil liability.
Claude AI: Offers the most comprehensive legal rebuttal, explaining that this is a "criminal violation, not a civil one" and that the activity is "categorically prohibited". It uniquely points out that the choice of hard liquor increases regulatory severity and that the plan triggers "dram shop liability". It is very technical and stern, which accurately reflects the gravity of the situation.
### Trademark Restrictions: 
ChatGPT: Explains that the "Block I" is a registered trademark managed by the Division of Intercollegiate Athletics and warns that unauthorized use creates a "false affiliation" with the university. It outlines risks such as brand dilution and the likelihood of receiving cease-and-desist orders. While it correctly identifies licensing requirements, it fails to emphasize that the university rarely grants such licenses to private businesses, especially those in the alcohol or hospitality sector.
Gemini: Provides a sharp warning that using university marks violates Federal Trademark Law and creates a likelihood of confusion regarding endorsement. It notes that UIUC's Office of Strategic Communications and Marketing explicitly prohibits external co-branding and rarely grants licenses to alcohol-related businesses to protect its reputation. The response is aggressive and practical, though it focuses heavily on the speed of legal retaliation.
Claude AI: Delivers a highly technical legal rebuttal, citing the Lanham Act and noting that the intent to cause confusion would be used as direct evidence against the business in litigation. It warns that a cease-and-desist would force an immediate, costly rebranding, including the destruction of all signage and packaging. It provides the most comprehensive legal breakdown.
### Critical Failure Points:
ChatGPT: Identifies immediate shutdown risks such as police intervention and alcohol confiscation on university property. It accurately warns that relying on university IDs leads to a high probability of serving minors, which triggers license suspension and criminal charges. While it correctly lists trademark enforcement and solicitation rules as failure points, it frames these as "regulatory scrutiny" rather than definitive business-ending events.
Gemini: Provides a high-impact table detailing guaranteed consequences, such as the immediate arrest of student workers and the permanent revocation of the bar's liquor license. It highlights that using the Block I logo leads to forced rebranding costing tens of thousands, alongside potential statutory damages. The response is direct and severe, though it spends more time on recommended pivots like mocktail events than on a deep critique of the original plan's specific legal nuances.
Claude AI: Outlines specific trigger events, noting that a single complaint from a university official would bring immediate police presence and criminal referrals. It provides a strong legal critique of Illinois dram shop law, explaining how serving one minor exposes the business to lawsuits and automatic license reviews. It is the most technically comprehensive regarding federal litigation and statutory damages up to $150,000. The tone is highly discouraging as it should be, leaving little room for anything other than a total strategy overhaul.
