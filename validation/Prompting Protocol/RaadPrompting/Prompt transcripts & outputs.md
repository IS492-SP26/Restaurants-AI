# Prompt 1 — Typical Case (Market Awareness)
### Purpose: Evaluate the AI's ability to analyze local market demand and identify specific competitor branding.
### Prompt:
I want to open a Chinese restaurant on Green Street in Champaign, IL, near the University of Illinois. My budget is $120,000 and my target customers are students.
Please analyze:
1.	The local demand for Chinese cuisine specifically among the UIUC international student population vs. domestic students.
2.	The brand positioning of current competitors on Green Street (e.g., Mid-Summer Lounge, Lao Sze Chuan) and where a "gap" exists.
3.	Whether a "Fast-Casual" or "Traditional Sit-Down" brand identity is more feasible for this location.
Please structure your answer in three sections: Market Demand, Competitors, and Feasibility Assessment.

# Outputs
## Gemini
# UIUC Green Street Restaurant Strategy - 2026

## 1. Market Demand: International vs. Domestic
The UIUC market is bifurcated, and your success depends on which group you prioritize as your "anchor" customer.

* **International Students (High Loyalty, High Standard):**
    * **The Nuance:** This group accounts for roughly **15–18%** of the student body, with a significant majority from China. They demand "hometown" authenticity (regional flavors like Szechuan, Hunan, or Cantonese) and are less sensitive to higher price points if the quality is genuine.
    * **The Trend:** There is a growing demand for **regional niche** foods (e.g., specialized rice noodles or "street food" snacks) rather than broad-menu "Chinese food."
* **Domestic Students (High Volume, Price Sensitive):**
    * **The Nuance:** Domestic students make up the bulk of the population. Their demand is driven by **convenience, speed, and value.**
    * **The Trend:** This group is increasingly moving toward "healthy-ish" Asian fusion or customizable bowls (the "Chipotle-fication" of Asian cuisine). They are less concerned with authenticity and more concerned with getting a **$12–$15** meal in under 10 minutes.

---

## 2. Competitors and Gap Analysis
Green Street is saturated with "Generalist" and "High-End" Chinese options. To survive, you must find the **"Missing Middle."**

| Competitor | Positioning | The "Gap" |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lao Sze Chuan** | Premium, traditional sit-down. High price point. | **Affordability:** Too expensive for a Tuesday night study break. |
| **Mid-Summer Lounge** | Specialized (Authentic Noodles). Mid-range. | **Customization:** Fixed menu items with less "build-your-own" flexibility. |
| **Panda Express** | Fast food, American-Chinese. Low price. | **Authenticity:** Domestic students want "real" flavors without the sit-down price. |

> **The Opportunity: "Authentic Fast-Casual"**
> There is a distinct gap for a **Single-Item Specialist** that uses authentic flavors but a fast-casual delivery system. Think "Malatang" (spicy hot pot cups), "Jianbing" (savory crepes), or "Bao" shops. These offer the authenticity international students crave with the speed and price point ($11–$14) that domestic students require.

---

## 3. Feasibility Assessment

### Financial Feasibility: Caution Advised
A **$120,000 budget** is extremely tight for Green Street.

* **Rent:** Current rates for prime Green Street frontage range from **$30 to $40 per sq. ft.** A small 1,200 sq. ft. space will cost ~$4,000/month plus NNN.
* **Build-out:** Even a "second-generation" space will likely eat **$60k–$80k** in equipment, signage, and cosmetic refreshes.
* **Reserve:** You need at least **$30k** in working capital for the first 3 months.

### Brand Identity: Fast-Casual is the Only Path
A "Traditional Sit-Down" model is **not feasible** for your budget or location.

* **Labor Costs:** Sit-down requires servers, which adds massive overhead.
* **Turnover:** Rent is paid by the square foot; you need high volume and fast turnover to survive.
* **Student Habits:** Students prefer "grab-and-go" or delivery via apps (DoorDash/Chowbus), making a large dining room a wasted expense.

---

## Final Recommendation
**Go with a "Micro-Concept" Fast-Casual Brand.** Focus on one or two high-quality, authentic "street food" items that can be prepared quickly. This minimizes equipment costs, reduces labor, and fits the high-velocity nature of Campustown.
