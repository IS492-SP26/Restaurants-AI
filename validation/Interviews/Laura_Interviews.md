# Interview Record – AI Co-founder Project

**Interviewee:** Entrepreneur with experience in restaurant planning and arcade (claw machine) business  
**Interviewer:** Laura  
**Purpose:** Understand the challenges founders face when starting a business and identify gaps where an AI co-founder tool could provide support.

---

## Q1. I heard that you previously considered starting a restaurant business but later paused the plan. What happened?

**Response:**

Initially, Mr. Hui was supposed to be the main investor for the project. However, he later changed his mind and said he could no longer invest that much money. At the beginning, we were considering franchising a relatively well-known hot pot restaurant brand. The franchise fee required a significant amount of capital. Once the investment funding was no longer available, we had to pause the entire plan.

---

## Q2. How far had the project progressed at that time? Did you encounter any difficulties during the process?

**Response:**

At that time we were also considering creating our own hot pot brand instead of franchising, so we had started experimenting with hot pot soup bases. We also began looking for potential locations. However, since the funding problem occurred early in the process, we had not progressed very far yet, so we did not encounter many operational difficulties at that stage.

---

## Q3. Later you started a business in the gaming industry by opening a claw machine arcade. Could you share your experience? How did it start, and what challenges did you face?

**Response:**

The decision to start the claw machine arcade was actually quite simple. There was not much deep analysis or hesitation before making the decision. At that time, claw machine arcades were becoming very popular, and many people wanted to play, but there were still very few stores available. It was clearly a trending business opportunity.

A close friend invited me to join the project, and in total there were six partners involved. One person acted as the leader of the group. He had prior entrepreneurial experience and was already operating a bar, so he handled most of the company registration procedures and administrative processes. Because he took on the main responsibilities, we gave him an additional share of ownership.

---

## Q4. Did you encounter any regulatory issues during the process? For example, consulting experts or facing rules that affected operations?

**Response:**

At the time when we started the claw machine business, it was still a relatively new and emerging industry. There were not many regulations initially. Later, some regulations gradually appeared. For example, stores were not allowed to open within a certain distance from primary or secondary schools.

---

## Q5. If you could go back to the beginning of the startup process, what kind of support or help would you most want?

**Response:**

Opening the claw machine arcade was actually relatively simple, so operationally it was manageable. The most important thing was having hardworking partners. The machines were usually delivered at night, and they were very heavy, so several people had to work together to move and install them.

---

## Q6. During the entrepreneurial process, what was the most difficult challenge you faced?

**Response:**

The most difficult part was communication among partners. My ideas often differed from the leader’s ideas, and it was sometimes difficult to reach agreement. For example, we had disagreements about who should manage the store, how working hours should be allocated, and how responsibilities should be divided. In the end, most decisions were made by the leader.

# Interview Record – AI Co-founder Project

**Interviewee:** Factory manager at a mobile phone circuit board manufacturing company  
**Interviewer:** Laura  
**Purpose:** Understand insights from industry experience related to business operations and decision-making, even if the interviewee has not personally started a company.

---

## Q1. Have you ever considered starting your own business?

**Response:**

I have never seriously started my own business before. Most of my career has been in manufacturing companies, and over time I moved into a management role. However, through my work I have interacted with many suppliers and clients who run their own companies, so I have been able to observe how they operate and make decisions.

From what I’ve seen, starting a manufacturing business usually requires a lot of capital and strong connections with both suppliers and customers. Because of that, many people with experience in the industry still choose to stay in management positions rather than take the risk of starting their own company.

---

## Q2. From your experience in manufacturing, what do you think is the biggest challenge when starting a business?

**Response:**

From my perspective, the biggest challenge is uncertainty. Even if someone has strong technical knowledge, running a business involves much more than just technical skills. You have to deal with finances, customer relationships, suppliers, and sometimes unexpected production problems.

I have seen many engineers who are very good at designing products or improving manufacturing processes, but running a company requires a completely different skill set. Because of that, being technically strong does not automatically mean someone will succeed as an entrepreneur.

---

## Q3. In your work experience, have you observed companies making mistakes during their early stages?

**Response:**

Yes, I have seen some common mistakes. One thing I noticed is that some startups focus too much on the product itself and not enough on the market. In manufacturing, developing a product is only one part of the process. The bigger challenge is finding stable customers and maintaining long-term orders.

I have seen companies invest a lot of money in equipment and technology, but later they realized they didn’t have enough orders to support their operations. When that happens, the financial pressure becomes very heavy and some companies cannot continue.

---

## Q4. What skills or qualities do you think are most important for someone who wants to start a business?

**Response:**

In my opinion, communication is very important. When running a business, you have to work with partners, employees, suppliers, and customers. If communication is not clear, small problems can easily turn into bigger issues.

Another important thing is patience. Especially in manufacturing, results usually do not come immediately. Building trust with customers and partners takes time.

---

## Q5. From your perspective, what kind of tools or support could help new entrepreneurs?

**Response:**

I think many new entrepreneurs struggle because they do not have enough information in the early stages. They have to make many decisions quickly, but sometimes they don’t know where to find reliable information or how to compare different options.

Tools that can help analyze information, summarize risks, or compare different strategies could be very useful. For example, a system that helps evaluate market demand, estimate costs, or highlight possible risks could help founders make better decisions.

---

## Q6. If someone wanted to start a business today, what advice would you give them?

**Response:**

My advice would be to spend more time researching before making large investments. Understanding the market, potential customers, and operational costs is very important.

I also think having trustworthy partners makes a big difference. Starting a business alone can be very difficult, so having partners with different strengths can help share responsibilities and reduce risks.
