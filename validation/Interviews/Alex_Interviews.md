## **Interviewee: Albert Chen (KPMG legal specialist)**

**1\. Please briefly talk about your role and what you have been working on:**

I currently work as a tax and compliance specialist at KPMG, where I focus on corporate tax compliance and helping businesses navigate complex regulatory frameworks. Prior to this, I worked as a Legal Specialist at Evershine CPA Firm and its affiliates.

**2\. Please talk about your experience helping companies be registered or helping companies handle business-relevant compliance:**

I have direct experience assisting with company formation projects across multiple international jurisdictions, specifically in Taiwan, Korea, and Vietnam. A significant part of my background involves managing comprehensive tax-exemption applications, navigating Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs), and ensuring compliance with specific regulations, such as provision 25-1 of the Income Tax Act. I’m familiar with how specific and localized business registration and compliance laws actually are.

**3\. Please talk about how AI and AI agents have affected your workplace:**

AI has definitely entered the industry as a tool for initial research, summarizing dense regulatory updates, and drafting standard communications. However, in the tax and legal compliance space, it is strictly an assistant, not a decision-maker. The liability risk of an AI hallucinating a tax code or missing a localized municipal law is too high, so every output requires heavy human auditing.

**4\. Please talk about how our multi-agent tool would help your client:**

Clients often bring us business plans that are conceptually great but completely ignore the hyper-local costs of compliance, zoning, and tax structures. Your multi-agent tool would be highly valuable because it forces early-stage entrepreneurs to confront these realities before they spend money. If your system can automatically link a client's operational plan with a localized compliance agent, they will be much better prepared and budgeted by the time they actually sit down with a professional.

**5\. What do you think about the existing drawbacks or gaps that these AI agents provide that need to be addressed (or potentially can be addressed by our multi-agentic approach):**

The biggest drawback of current AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude is that they operate in a vacuum. They give generic, liability-dodging advice and don't enforce dependencies. For example, if an entrepreneur slashes their startup budget, a standard AI might just adjust the spreadsheet without realizing that the new budget no longer covers mandatory health permits or commercial grease traps. Your multi-agent approach solves this by having specialized agents (like a Critic or Finance agent) actively challenge the operational plan, mimicking the cross-domain pushback you'd get from an actual legal or financial team.

**Gap Analysis of current tools**

**1\. Failure to Enforce Cross-Domain Dependencies:** Current AI tools operate in a vacuum and lack internal system logic. If a user drastically cuts their startup budget, the AI will often adjust the spreadsheet without realizing that the new budget can no longer cover mandatory localized costs, such as health permits or grease traps.

**2\. Model Hallucinations:** Single-prompt LLMs act as "people pleasers" rather than reliable decision-makers. Instead of telling a user that a restrictive budget is impossible, they will often hallucinate a way to make it seem feasible. Additionally, the advice they provide is often generic and lacks industrial best practices.

**3\. Context Degradation on Complex Tasks:** Attempting to generate a massive, complex business plan all at once through a single prompt causes the AI to suffer from high latency and context degradation.
