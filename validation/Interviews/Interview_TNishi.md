#Interview 2: (Given same question)
##Interviewee: Restaurant Business Consultant specializing in new restaurant launches and operational restructuring in Japan

Question and Answers (Translated in English)

**1. Currently, if a person were to set up a food business from scratch, what sort of information would they struggle to acquire? Or often underestimate?**

One of the most common things new founders underestimate is the time and complexity involved in preparing a realistic business plan. Many people focus heavily on their menu concept or interior design, but they do not spend enough time estimating realistic revenue projections or operating costs.

Another area that is often overlooked is regulatory compliance and licensing requirements. Depending on the type of restaurant, owners may need multiple permits related to food safety, alcohol service, fire safety, and building regulations. These requirements vary by municipality and can delay the opening if not prepared in advance.

**2. There are certainly examples where a shop can fail despite its services and quality of food being good. What are some of the common causes for this?**

In many cases, failure occurs due to poor strategic positioning rather than poor food quality. For example, the restaurant may not clearly differentiate itself from nearby competitors, making it difficult to attract repeat customers.

Another common issue is insufficient capital planning. Some founders assume that once the restaurant opens, revenue will quickly stabilize. In reality, many restaurants take several months to reach stable customer demand. Without sufficient financial reserves, businesses can run into cash flow problems early on.

**3. In the food business, are there any tasks you believe AI can handle better than a human worker? Additionally, do you have any reservations about certain tasks being handled by AI?**

AI can be very effective in analyzing large amounts of operational data, such as sales trends, customer purchasing patterns, and cost fluctuations. This type of analysis can help owners make better decisions about menu design, pricing, and promotions.

However, I think AI should be used as a decision-support tool rather than a decision-maker. Restaurants often depend on subtle factors such as atmosphere, customer relationships, and local community dynamics. These are difficult for AI systems to fully understand.

**4. Assuming a founder successfully launches a store, how can Generative AI and AI tools help further expand the business?**

AI tools can support expansion by helping businesses evaluate new location opportunities and market segments. For example, AI can combine demographic data, tourism statistics, and consumer behavior patterns to estimate potential demand in different areas.

Additionally, AI can help with brand development and customer engagement, such as generating marketing content, managing online reviews, or analyzing customer feedback from social media and reservation platforms.

**5. What do you think are the greatest challenges a new owner or existing owners face in the current food industry?**

From a consulting perspective, I see two major challenges.

First is operational sustainability. Many restaurant owners work extremely long hours, and maintaining consistent service quality over time can be difficult without strong management systems.

Second is industry volatility. The food industry is very sensitive to external factors such as economic conditions, tourism trends, and changes in ingredient prices. Owners need to constantly adapt their strategy in order to remain competitive.

**6. Based on the 3 principles AI agents we have (marketing, finance, and legal expert), do you believe these are enough to cover the opening or continued operation of a store? (Do you have any additional advice to improve the framework?)**

The three agents you described cover several essential areas for starting a business. Marketing helps identify the target market, finance supports cost planning and profitability analysis, and legal ensures compliance with regulations.

However, I would also recommend including an operations-focused perspective. Restaurants require constant adjustments in staffing, procurement, and service processes. An AI agent that focuses on operational efficiency and performance monitoring could provide valuable support for business owners.

At the same time, entrepreneurs should remember that AI tools are support systems rather than substitutes for managerial judgment. Successful restaurant owners still need to develop a deep understanding of their customers, employees, and local market environment.
